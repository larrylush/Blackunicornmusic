Sometimes It Clicks, Sometimes It Doesn’t

Over the years I’ve found myself in the studio with some people I’d been listening to for years. I’d go in thinking we might make something special. Sometimes we did. Sometimes the most useful thing I took home was a better question to ask at the start of the next session.

By Larry Lush

I worked on quite a few remixes with Daniele Davoli, one of the people behind Black Box’s “Ride on Time”. At one point we did a version of k.d. lang’s “Constant Craving”. I had high hopes for it, but it never came to anything. It’s funny how much work can go into a record that never becomes a record anyone can buy.

Then there was an overnight session with Arthur Baker. I was there as his engineer. I was tired, a little nervous, and very aware that I was sitting next to Arthur Baker. I couldn’t quite work out what he wanted from me, or how much he wanted me to bring to the session. Looking back, I could have just asked. At the time that felt like the hardest thing in the room.

Nothing came of that session either. Remixes come in, you have a go, and for all sorts of reasons some of them never quite work. I’ve learned to take each day as it comes and give the next piece of music a fair chance. A session that goes nowhere is part of the job. You can leave it at the studio and come back the next day.

Sometimes the magic is there and sometimes it isn’t. That’s the essence of making things. You turn up, listen, and see what happens.

Daniele Davoli on Black Box and “Ride on Time” · k.d. lang’s original “Constant Craving”
