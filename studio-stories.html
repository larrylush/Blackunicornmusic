How our Blur and Prodigy bootleg went from white label to Parlophone

That’s me in the suit on the front cover. Dave Conway and I had invented a bloke called Brock Landars, an American porn star who’d made a record. That was the ruse. We made the record, and I got to play Brock on the sleeve.

By Larry Lush

When Dave Conway, who ran Silver Planet Records, and I made this record, Blur’s “Song 2” and the Prodigy’s “Smack My Bitch Up” were both everywhere. I remember talk of bad blood between the bands. Whether there was anything in it or it was just the way the story was told at the time, there was something funny about putting them on the same record.

We took the break from the Prodigy record and put Blur’s “Song 2” over it, “woo-hoo” and all. We called it “Smack My Dick Up” and made up an artist name, Brock Landars. It started circulating as a white label. London’s Xfm got a copy and kept playing it.

Then Parlophone picked it up. The label that had released “Song 2” was now putting out the record we’d made from it, licensed from Silver Planet. It came out in 1998 as S.M.D.U., still under the Brock Landars name, with “Crazy Sadie” alongside it. It reached No. 49 on the UK Singles Chart that July.

The trade press even printed a backstory for Brock, the American porn star we’d invented, complete with a gambling debt. Dave and I just made the record. By the time it charted, the bloke we’d made up had a life of his own.

Coldcut’s Autumn Leaves with Mixmaster Morris

Mixmaster Morris and I made a remix of Coldcut’s “Autumn Leaves” together. Years later, I saw Norman Cook name it as his all-time favourite chillout record.

By Larry Lush

Morris was close to Matt Black and the Ninja Tune crew. Coldcut had made their version of “Autumn Leaves”, and Morris and I worked together on what became the Irresistible Force mix. The Irresistible Force was Morris’s recording name, and that’s the name you’ll see on the remix.

I saw the interview on YouTube. There was Norman Cook, better known as Fatboy Slim, picking our mix as his all-time favourite chillout record. It’s a lovely thing to hear about a record you made with a friend.
